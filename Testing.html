Nº 03

Field Notes

Nº 12 — FEBRUARY, SENJA

We skinned up the west couloir in the blue hour, ropes coiled, the thermometer swinging between opinions. At the ridge the wind found the gap in my layers that the catalogue had promised did not exist. That is the thing about cold: it is an honest auditor. It finds the line you skipped, the seam you trusted, the mitten you left drying by the stove.

“Cold is an honest auditor. It finds the seam you trusted and the one you didn’t.”

By noon the sky had hardened into that particular Arctic silver that makes distances lie. We navigated by compass and stubbornness in equal measure. The Kompass 54 never blinked — liquid-damped, patient, indifferent to weather in the way only good instruments are.

We were down by four, headlamps making small theaters of the snow. In the hut I wrote the day into the logbook, boiled snow for tea, and repaired a torn wind flap with sailcloth and dental floss, as one does. The kit came home wet, scorched at the cuffs, and completely correct.
